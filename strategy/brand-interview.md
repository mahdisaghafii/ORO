# Instagram Tech Influencer Strategy Interview — Export

## Context

The user wants to start an Instagram page targeting an Iranian audience. The initial idea was around:
- How to make USD income from Iran
- How to make income using tools
- Teaching AI tools
- Design / technology
- Becoming a tech influencer

The goal of the interview was to discover the user's authentic positioning, audience, content strategy, brand identity, and long-term business direction.

---

# 1. Desired Reputation

The user wants people to think:

> “This is the guy I follow when I want to…”

- Learn new skills or apps
- Get updates
- Discover ways to make income
- Learn how to start a business and automate it
- Learn how to use the best tools in business
- Learn how to make money and start a tech business more easily
- Learn skills that let them work internationally

Key insight:
The user does not want to be limited to being an “AI influencer.” AI is a vehicle. The broader territory is helping Iranians become more capable through technology, find opportunities, improve businesses, and build things.

The user described these strengths:

- Long experience in DM and PM / product management
- Worked in international businesses
- Built several online businesses
- Can teach well and enjoys teaching
- Can combine AI and tools to make better things
- Knows how to build
- Knows how to create systems
- Critical thinker
- Pragmatist
- Good taste in UX and understanding user problems
- Strong with data

The user's resume adds significant evidence:

- 7+ years across fintech, algorithmic trading, Web3 gaming, AI products, and SaaS
- Built two engineering organizations from scratch
- Currently owns strategy/delivery across four concurrent products
- Operates as a technical product owner, including API/data specifications, architecture, automation workflows, lifecycle/revenue metrics, and debugging integrations
- Built an 8-person cross-functional engineering team from zero
- Worked on AI products, automation, BI, product strategy, growth, UX, SaaS, trading systems
- Built automation with n8n and Node-RED
- Built dashboards using Grafana, Metabase, and ClickHouse
- Has experience with customer lifecycle, ARPU, ARPPU, retention, funnels and revenue performance

Product + UX + Business + Data + Automation + AI + Marketing + Systems + Growth + Technology.

# 4. Target Audience

Primary audience:

- Mostly age 18–27
- Many are students
- Some are current employees
- Some are business owners
- Ambitious people looking for a mentor/role model they can trust
- Often don't know where to start
- Feel lost among too many opportunities
- Want to improve their current situation
- Want to make money or make their time more valuable
- Know how to use a phone and may have a laptop
- Spend time on work or education
- Interested in opportunities and improvement

Psychographic insight:

The audience is not necessarily looking specifically for AI.

They are more broadly asking:

> “What should I learn?”
> “Where should I start?”
> “What opportunity should I pursue?”
> “How can I become more valuable?”
> “How can I improve my current situation?”
> “What can I actually build?”

The user wants to be someone they can trust.

The user is unsure of the exact audience “wow moment” and wants to discover it through content/testing rather than assuming it.

# 7. Natural Teaching Style

When asked what they would tell a 20-year-old in Iran who has no money, no programming skills and no career direction, the user naturally answered:

1. Find out what you're good at
2. If you don't know, take a psychology test
3. Pick a field based on:
   - What you're good at
   - Income available in the market
   - What you love
4. Find a learning path
5. Spend at least six months learning hard
6. Apply for an internship, even if unpaid
7. Get a junior role
8. Progress toward mid/senior
9. Eventually start a business if desired
10. The hardest part is the beginning

Important insight:
The user naturally thinks in PATHS and DIAGNOSIS, not isolated tips.

A possible improvement to the philosophy:
Instead of only giving linear six-month paths, teach people how to test whether a field is worth six months before committing to it.

# 11. Iran-Specific Constraints

The user highlighted a major problem with international income:

When an employer/customer discovers that the other party is Iranian, they may refuse to buy/work with them.

Also:
- Payment methods are limited
- Some websites have eligibility/KYC/verification restrictions
- Some international opportunities are inaccessible due to nationality/location/payment constraints

Therefore the user does NOT want the brand to simply tell Iranians:
> “Go freelance on X platform.”

The strategy needs to find opportunities that are not dependent on inaccessible platforms/payment/KYC structures.

Important strategic principle:

> Do not build the brand around circumventing KYC, hiding Iranian identity, fake residency, borrowed accounts, or other platform/compliance workarounds.

Instead investigate opportunities where the value creation model itself is more compatible with the user's constraints.

Potential directions:
- Building software/products
- Digital products
- Global audiences
- Productized technology
- B2B problem solving
- Local businesses → technology solutions → productization
- International opportunities where location/payment constraints are not the core blocker
- Open-source / developer ecosystem opportunities
- Content/education where the business model is viable
- Building global products rather than relying entirely on foreign employment platforms

# 23. Key Strategic Warning

Do NOT make the entire identity:

> “How to make USD from Iran.”

Reason:
- It can attract low-trust, low-quality “get rich quick” audiences.
- It can trap the brand into side-hustle content.
- Many opportunities have genuine Iran-specific KYC/payment constraints.
- It doesn't represent the full range of the user's expertise.
- It could conflict with the long-term goal of building software/tools.

Better:

> “Technology, opportunities, and building.”

Then USD income becomes an important content theme/outcome.
